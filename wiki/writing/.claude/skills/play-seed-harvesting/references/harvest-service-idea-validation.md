# Harvest Service: Selective Idea Validation

## Trigger and authority

Load only when the user explicitly considers the seed-harvesting system as a service, product, paid offering, or reusable workflow for other writers. This is a method for testing that possibility, not approval to build or launch it. Do not load for routine harvesting, A1 adjudication, or creative selection alone.

Keep three questions independent:

1. **Collection/judgment validity:** Are sources accessible, provenance intact, and A0/A1 judgments supported?
2. **Writer value:** Does the service help a particular writer find something they want to follow, with less unwanted research or rework and without losing agency?
3. **Operating viability:** Can it be delivered repeatedly with acceptable labor, cost, source rights, privacy protection, and support burden?

A1 pass rate is not product-market fit. Payment is not literary merit. A writer's refusal to use an A1-valid seed is not automatically a judge error. Market preference must not become an A0 topic veto or change A1 criteria.

## Start with one falsifiable hypothesis

Use the existing project decision note or a compact experiment record; do not create a parallel product-management bureaucracy. If an audience or price is unknown, mark it as a hypothesis, not a fact.

Record:

- Target segment and actual job: who, at what writing/research moment, with what constraints.
- Current alternative: self-search, saved links, newsletters, existing tools, or doing nothing; include real time and rework where observable.
- Proposed value: which part becomes easier, or which new creative possibility becomes available. Do not assume speed is the only value.
- Riskiest assumption: value, usability, feasibility, or viability; choose one primary question per experiment.
- Disconfirming observation: what would make us hold, change, or stop rather than explain away the result.
- Bounded test: audience, task, sample/output limit, duration, budget, source permissions, consent and storage/retention boundary.
- Evidence and decision rule fixed before results: what to observe, denominator, baseline/comparator, continue/change/stop conditions, and known limitations.

Do not invent universal respondent counts, conversion targets, savings ratios, or price points. Agree context-specific criteria before recruitment or data collection.

## Choose the cheapest honest test

| Uncertainty | Preferred test | Useful evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Is the problem real? | Listening: discuss a recent concrete writing/research episode | Actual workaround, lost effort, unmet need; not agreement with a sales pitch |
| Does the output help? | Manual/concierge delivery of a small rights-cleared seed packet | What the writer revisits, rejects, transforms, or asks to receive again, with reasons |
| Can the writer use it unaided? | Narrow prototype with a representative task | Independent task completion, confusion, review burden, need for explanation |
| Can it operate sustainably? | Costed repeat-delivery rehearsal | Review/repair time, source availability, privacy handling, support and marginal delivery costs |
| Is there demand beyond courtesy? | Consented follow-up and repeat-use observation | Voluntary return, specific new request, referral, or explicit willingness to pay, distinguished from actual payment |

Default to listening or transparent manual delivery before adding a backend, new source pools, automation, accounts, billing, or a polished product. Explain that the pilot is manually assisted; do not imply a working autonomous service that does not exist. Test one uncertainty at a time, changing only what the experiment needs.

## Measure behavior without turning writing into a funnel

- Ask about past behavior before hypothetical desire: what did the writer last search for, keep, abandon, or use, and why?
- Record polite praise, stated intent, observed use, repeat use, unsolicited request/referral, and actual paid demand as different evidence classes. A waitlist or willingness-to-pay answer alone does not prove usage or payment.
- Separate friends/known collaborators from independently recruited participants and separate writer segments. Self-use is valuable dogfooding, not evidence of broad demand.
- Compare the pilot against the writer's existing method on a comparable task. Capture time to a personally worth-following question, review/rework burden, provenance trust, unwanted homogenization, and autonomy as relevant. Completion of a play or commercial success is not a short-pilot requirement.
- Distinguish technical yield (`opened`, A0 residue, A1 verdict) from user outcomes (voluntary selection, revisit, independent continuation) and business outcomes (repeat demand, paid demand, delivery cost).
- Do not add hidden tracking or collect drafts, keystrokes, personal biographies, or client manuscripts by default. Use consented minimal observations; participants may decline to share creative work.
- Show non-use, rejection, dropouts and mixed results, with denominators and uncertainty. Small convenience pilots cannot estimate the whole writing market. Document competing explanations such as novelty, facilitator effort, or social obligation.

## Rights and publication boundary

The private-workshop A1 full-view convention does **not** authorize distributing community post bodies, source receipts, identifiable stories, or the user's Vault to service customers.

Before any external pilot, separately confirm source access, lawful use/redistribution, privacy, participant consent, and the exact deliverable. Prefer original transformed observations/questions plus permitted attribution and links; these still need source-specific rights and re-identification checks. Public accessibility, robots permission, and anonymization alone do not grant redistribution rights. Use licensed or participant-authorized material when public redistribution is unclear; hold the external test rather than smuggle private-workshop data into a product.

Keep raw receipts, evaluation traces and participant data outside Vault/Git and outside public demos. Do not use private user material for training or external model processing without explicit scope and consent. Keep the user-facing private A1 ledger intact under its existing rules; do not globally hide sources just because service output has a different boundary.

Drafting a test plan does not authorize outreach, participant recruitment, advertisements, posting a landing page, accepting payment, sending invoices, collecting credentials, or sharing content. Obtain explicit approval for the concrete action, audience, content, and budget. Do not import deceptive fake-door tests or pre-product invoice tactics as defaults.

## Close the experiment without automatic expansion

Use one compact record:

`hypothesis → baseline → scoped test → observed behavior and counts → disconfirming evidence → delivery cost/rights limits → continue / change / hold / stop → next approval needed`

- Keep observation separate from inference; preserve failed and neutral cases.
- If value is weak, revisit segment/problem/output before building more features.
- If value exists but delivery requires excessive human repair or uncertain rights, address that bottleneck before scaling collection.
- Reuse a costed manual workflow only after its value repeats; automation is a subsequent decision, not the purpose of validation.
- Any new source, larger crawl, revised adjudication rubric, external sharing, paid pilot, or launch requires its own authorization. Commercial demand cannot override source ethics, writing-first selection, or an existing hold.
